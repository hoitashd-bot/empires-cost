Field manual

How to Play

Objective

Lead a European power from 1884 to 1914 across 12 turns. Expand your empire and its wealth while the game records the human cost of every decision. The final report scores territory, economy, military record and historical knowledge separately from human cost and political consequences.

The map

  • Hover over a territory for a summary. Click it for the full dossier.
  • Drag to pan. Use the mouse wheel or the + and − buttons to zoom.
  • Hover over resource icons to learn why Europeans sought each resource.
  • Interior territories need a supply route through a territory you hold next to them. Coastal territories can be reached by sea.

Each turn

  • Buy preparations in the War Office: recruit troops, build supply lines, use diplomacy and gather intelligence. Each can be bought once per turn.
  • Then begin one campaign, or end the turn to collect income.
  • Every campaign uses up the turn. Rival powers make their historical claims as the years pass.

Battles

  • Each round, answer a true-or-false history question.
  • Correct: you get two better-informed options, such as supply lines, treaties or limited operations.
  • Incorrect or skipped: you get two riskier options.
  • Outcomes depend on your military power, the defender's strength, terrain, your preparation and your choices. Victory is never guaranteed.
  • You win when the defender's strength reaches 0 or a treaty is signed. You lose when your force reaches 0, your treasury runs out, or 8 rounds pass without a decisive result.

Strength and difficulty

The difficulty rating compares your military power, plus preparation bonuses, against the defender's strength adjusted for terrain. Weak powers that attack strong states without preparing will usually lose. Recruiting, supply lines, intelligence and diplomacy all improve your odds.

Human cost

The ledger tracks simulated casualties (African and colonial forces separately), displacement, forced labor, wealth extracted and sovereignty lost. The administration policy you choose after a conquest keeps causing harm, provoking resistance and setting off uprisings in later turns.
